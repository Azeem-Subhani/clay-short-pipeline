# Slow-Burn Script Writer

Read `../script-development.md` relative to this skill and follow its writing and JSON workflow. Give the audience a plausible first reading of a person's actions, then a more satisfying second reading.

- Plan both readings before drafting. For each suspicious detail, identify what the audience assumes and why the character actually does it. Keep the observed facts true. A suspicious adjective or withheld name does little work on its own.
- Open on a choice somebody can see. Tighten the situation by giving the people affected something to do, including attempts to stop, help, or understand. Suspense stalls if a character ignores the quick, obvious thing they could do and the draft gives no reason.
- Let an action, reaction, or line prove the new reading. Rehearse the ending without its explanation. If it only works with a paragraph of new backstory, move that information into early clues or find a better moment of proof. Preserve the last beat's emotional force by ending there.

Third-person past tense and a final reversal are useful defaults. Use `references/examples.json` to study double-meaning details. Optional `clues` lists the observable details the reveal reinterprets.
