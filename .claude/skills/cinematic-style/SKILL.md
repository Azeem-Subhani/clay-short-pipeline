# Cinematic style

The full guide is **`style/STYLE_GUIDE.md`**. Read it once at the start of your task; it is short and every section has examples. This page is the card you keep in front of you.

**One test:** does this make the viewer feel or understand exactly what the narrator's words need right now? If you cannot say why something is there, take it out.

## Before you cut, try

| The story needs… | Inside the shot |
|---|---|
| attention to move between two things in frame | shift focus |
| a feeling to grow | slow dolly in, 5–15% over the sentence |
| new information in the same place | crane, tilt or dolly to reveal it |
| a change of state | change the light on the word |
| a reaction | head turn, brows, a step, a stop |
| a line to land | hold: stop the camera completely |

Cut for a new place or time, a jump in scale a move cannot make, to skip the unbuildable part of an action, or for the twist's clean frame. About 4–5 shots per Short.

## Camera

- A move needs a motivation. One move at a time. Slow by default. Start before the word, land on it.
- Hold when the action or the light is the event, and always on the last image.
- Blur answers "does the place matter right now?": `light` to read a place, `medium` for a character in it, `strong` for a face or a clue.
- Never blur what the narrator is talking about. Focus on an object or `actor.head`, not a number.
- 9:16: stack the frame vertically, eyes at 45–80% height, keep key content inside the safe box, fill the top (sky, ceiling, tall walls).

## Light

- One rig per set and time of day: key from the side (35–80° off camera), soft fill always on, a rim at night, practicals for depth.
- Darkness is deep colour, not black. The subject is the brightest or most contrasted thing unless it is a deliberate silhouette.
- Everything touches what it rests on and throws a shadow.
- Light changes only when the story does, on the word.

## Staging

- Reliable: walking between marks, standing, sitting, lying, crouching, reaching, head turns, brows, blinks, a prop in a hand, rigid props moving, light and camera.
- Stage around: two characters touching in close-up, fingers, climbing, fighting, mouth acting (there is no mouth).
- Tricks: start–cut–result, let the object act, shadow or silhouette, one simple motion in a close-up, let sound carry it.
- Nothing freezes: every character breathes and blinks. Eyes lead the action by a third of a second.

## Review (five questions, in order)

1. Purpose: do the frames do what the plan says this shot is for?
2. First glance: does the eye land on what the narrator is talking about?
3. Mood and light: right feeling, subject readable, dark is coloured, consistent with the set's other shots?
4. Polish: anything floating, intersecting, stiff, empty, sharp-edged or off-scale?
5. Fair play and safety: nothing shown early, clues visible, key content in the safe area?

Smallest fix that serves the purpose. Never "add a cut". An action that does not read gets "restage".
