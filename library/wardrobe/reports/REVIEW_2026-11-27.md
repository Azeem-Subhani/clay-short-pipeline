## Common faults by outfit, and what would fix them

Faults shared by every outfit (garment maker):
- **Seat balloon, sit seen from behind.** The hem of any top that reaches the hips, or any skirt, bags into a round flap or bubble over the seat. Fix: collapse the hem and back panels toward the thigh bones in the sit pose (a corrective shape key or tighter weights on the hip and upper-leg bones), and shorten the back of tops to the hip line.
- **Skirts and tunic skirts in sit and stride.** Seated from the front they open into a wide disc or bowl around the knees (a dark hollow shows inside the peasant woman skirt); striding they fold against the forward leg. Fix: split skirt weights per leg with a stiffer front and back panel, and add a sit corrective that lifts the hem forward instead of flaring sideways.
- **Footwear.** Bootee blobs on the kid shorts set and the white sock shoes on the casual tee set have no sole or opening; the tall boots (guard, peasant man) are straight tubes. Fix: a simple sole and opening ring on the shoes.

By outfit:
- **casual_tshirt_trousers, shirt_trousers, kid_tshirt_shorts**: clean standing and moving; only the shared seat balloon and shoes. The shirt is untucked at hip length so the office read is modest (add a tucked variant or a hem that ends at the belt). Kid sleeves on shirt_trousers are puffed and end above the wrist with a round collar.
- **blouse_skirt**: fine on the four female bodies within limits. On male bodies it reads as a man or boy in a skirt (rejected): either keep it female-only in the registry or make a male-cut tunic. Speckles at the blouse back hem on Female_Senior.
- **dress_simple**: the top and skirt are separate pieces with a hard step at the waist, so it reads as a two-piece. On adult and senior women the bodice is lumpy over the bust and stomach and a loose fold stands out at the torso side (rejected). Fix: one continuous bodice-to-skirt surface or a waist band that hides the join; refit the bodice to the bust and waist.
- **king_robes**: crown and ermine yoke sell the king, but it is a knee tunic over trousers, so more prince than king. Fix: floor-length robe or mantle over the shoulders.
- **queen_gown** (rejected): same knee tunic as the king with trousers visible. Fix: a floor-length gown skirt covering the legs, with fitted bodice.
- **guard_tabard**: plain red sleeveless tunic with no emblem or helmet, so the role comes from props. Fix: an emblem on the chest and a helmet or cap; make the boot tops meet the trousers.
- **knight_armour** (rejected): glossy silver vest over grey padded sleeves reads as a puffer jacket or space suit. Fix: shoulder plates, a mail skirt or coat, helmet and gauntlets, and a matte metal material without the stretched highlights.
- **kitchen_maid, peasant_woman**: apron fix works from the front (white panel over a coloured skirt); from the side the panel meets the skirt in a hard vertical split, and trousers show beneath. Fix: wrap the apron round the hip sides, and use a longer skirt or remove the trousers.
- **peasant_man**: the best of the period outfits; only the shared seat balloon.
